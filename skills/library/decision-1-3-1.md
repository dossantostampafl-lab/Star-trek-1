When a task has several viable approaches and the Commander needs a decision, answer in the 1-3-1 shape: one problem, three options, one recommendation. It turns "it depends" into something they can act on or forward to a team.

## When to use
- They ask for a "1-3-1", "give me options", or "what are my choices".
- A real decision with meaningful trade-offs: architecture, tooling, migration path, refactor strategy.
- NOT for questions with one obvious answer, or when they've already decided — just do it then.

## The shape
1. **Problem** — one sentence. State the decision or desired outcome, the *what* not the *how*. If you need "and", that's two problems; split them.
2. **Options** — exactly three, labelled A/B/C. Each gets a one-line description, honest pros, honest cons. They must be genuinely different strategies, not tweaks of one idea.
3. **Recommendation** — pick ONE and say why, grounded in their context and priorities. Be direct; this is judgment, not a hedge.
4. **Definition of done** — concrete, verifiable success criteria for the recommended path.
5. **Plan** — the specific steps to execute it (commands, files, actions where they apply).

If the Commander picks a different option, revise the recommendation, done-criteria, and plan to match it.

## Check yourself
Exactly one problem sentence. Exactly three options, each with pros AND cons. One recommendation that commits. Done-criteria and plan align with the recommended option.

*Pure reasoning — needs no workstation objects.*
