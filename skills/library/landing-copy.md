A landing page fails when it describes the product instead of answering the visitor's questions in the order they actually arrive. The structure does most of the work; the wordsmithing does far less than people assume.

## Method
1. **Pin ONE audience and ONE action.** A page serving three audiences converts none of them. If there are genuinely three, that is three pages.
2. **Write the above-the-fold block to survive five seconds:** what this is, who it is for, what changes for them, and the action. A visitor who reads only the headline should be able to say what you do.
3. **Lead with the outcome, not the mechanism.** People buy the change; the clever implementation is proof, not the pitch.
4. **Answer objections in arrival order** — is this for me, will it actually work, how much effort is it, what does it cost, what if it goes wrong. Each objection gets its own block, in that sequence.
5. **Use proof you actually have.** Real numbers, real quotes, a real demo, a real guarantee. Where there is none, say less rather than inflating.
6. **Read what the audience actually says** with web_search / fetch_url — reviews, forum complaints, competitor pages. Their words convert better than yours; note where you borrowed them.
7. **One primary action, repeated.** Same wording every time, and remove every competing link that leads away.
8. **Cut ruthlessly at the end.** Most drafts lose a third with no loss of meaning. Save with write_file.

## Rules
- **Never invent a testimonial, a customer count, a rating, or a result.** One fabricated proof point poisons the whole page.
- **No fake scarcity or countdown** the Commander will not honour.
- **Every claim must be substantiable** — flag anything that needs evidence before it can ship.
- Say plainly when the honest fix is the product or the offer, not the copy.

## Output
The page section by section with the reasoning for each, the objections in arrival order, the proof used and the proof still needed, then the lines you cut.
